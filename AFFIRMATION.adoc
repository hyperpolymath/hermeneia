== What this is, and how it works

A short, dated, signed snapshot of what hyperpolymath can honestly and verifiably
claim about *Hermeneia* at one exact commit. Nothing here is marketing or a
promise; those live in the README. This file is the receipt. Every claim below
was produced by running the project's own checks in the session that wrote this
file; the anchor (<<verifiable-anchor>>) names the commit, time and toolchain, so
"true" means "true at this point". It is to be landed by a signed commit — the
owner signs on landing.

== The epistemic contract

This records hyperpolymath's best belief at the timestamp below, not a guarantee
of correctness. The only guarantee is no intentional overclaim: proved is called
proved, a trust boundary is called a trust boundary, an unimplemented verb is
called unimplemented. You are invited to refute any line with a counter-example,
a failing run, or a contradicting source.

== What we do NOT claim

* `evoke`, `convoke`, `transvoke`, `provoke`, `intervoke`, `revoke` — recognised by
  keyword and refused as unimplemented (their bodies are not parsed); the refusal is
  what is tested (`docs/SPEC.adoc`).
* `show loss` and `show warrant` — refused (`hermeneia-core::plan_invoke`); the first
  needs the checker's loss vector, the second the warrant graph. `show failure_points`
  is parsed but *not* refused by the planner — the request proceeds to the checker —
  although `docs/SPEC.adoc` lists it as refused; that line is stale (finding, not fixed
  here).
* Nothing is Integrated: the store is a stub (`hermeneia-store`), the Vocarium
  `StoreProvider` adapter (M2) does not exist yet.
* No proofs of our own: the grade algebra is the checker's, machine-checked
  there (`trope-checker/docs/status/PROOF-STATUS.adoc`), cited not restated.
